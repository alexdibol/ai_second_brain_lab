The Second Brain

Knowledge is an architecture of judgment.

A model can only reason over the context it receives. In complex institutions, the decisive information is spread across sources, jurisdictions, relationships, and time.

The project studies how portable notes, typed knowledge graphs, explicit provenance, temporal validity, and decision-specific retrieval can help assemble a more useful and auditable view of a problem.

01 · REPRESENT

Make knowledge addressable.

Convert documents into navigable notes while retaining source passages, dates, status, and stable identifiers.

02 · CONNECT

Give relationships meaning.

Use typed, evidence-backed links to expose dependencies, authority chains, conflicts, and multi-hop paths.

03 · ASSEMBLE

Build context for a decision.

Retrieve relevant evidence and uncertainty within explicit limits. Let people inspect how the dossier was formed.

LAB 1
Authority & constraints

Regulatory intelligence during a payment-system crisis

In a synthetic crisis, determine which measures fit an authority’s competence, which require coordination or legislation, and what residual risks remain. The vault connects legal sources, provisions, institutions, activities, and proposed interventions.

Can the measure be supported by a traceable authority chain?

LAB 2
Economic transmission

Trading intelligence and rapid contagion

Trace how an operational or supply-chain shock could reach a synthetic universe of roughly 500 listed companies through supplier, financing, ownership, competition, and shared-infrastructure links. Graph analysis complements conventional risk measures and generates hypotheses for review.

Where might the shock travel before returns reveal it?

LAB 3
Strategic discovery

Multinational M&A opportunity discovery

Search a synthetic corporate universe for complementary capabilities, licenses, technologies, and distribution. Compare tabular, semantic, graph-guided, and hybrid methods while keeping strategic fit, regulatory feasibility, and transaction economics distinct.

Which target is attractive, explainable, and feasible?

LAB 4
Temporal institutional memory

Central-bank institutional memory

Organize synthetic policy episodes around contemporaneous evidence, alternatives, decisions, outcomes, dissent, and later revisions. Assess historical analogies without hindsight leakage, and show how new evidence should change—or leave unchanged—a committee brief.

What was known then, and what is genuinely comparable now?

Materials in development. The monograph describes the design. This repository does not yet contain the Lab 4 monograph, slides, or Colab notebook. View Lab 4 folder ↗
LAB 5
Bounded adaptive research

Autonomous legal research and endogenous graph navigation

The capstone uses a synthetic Mexican legal system and three incomplete consultations. The LLM proposes actions such as inspecting a record, following a typed relationship, searching a domain, testing an exception, or stopping. A deterministic gateway validates and executes each action, enforces budgets, updates the evidence state, and logs the trace.

Can adaptive research improve evidence discovery while keeping execution bounded and reviewable?

Monograph, slides & notebook available

Compare semantic retrieval, fixed graph traversal, and LLM-guided navigation. The output is a conditional, source-traceable memorandum for human review—not an autonomous legal opinion.

The conceptual synthesis

Test the value
of structure.

The project does not assume that a graph or an agent is always better. It compares information architectures on the same task and evidence wherever practicable.

The monograph sets out the shared architecture, experimental design, evaluation criteria, and intended notebook roadmap for all five labs.

EVALUATE, DO NOT PRESUME

Structure is a hypothesis.

Completeness, traceability, responsiveness, and decision quality are evaluated against meaningful baselines. A well-maintained long-context or semantic system may be sufficient for many questions.

KEEP THE EVIDENCE VISIBLE

Paths are not proof.

Graph links and model outputs require source validation. Errors in a corpus, edge, or research policy can make a system’s mistakes more systematic.

Alejandro Reynoso

This project develops a practical teaching framework for professionals who need to understand how AI systems use knowledge, navigate evidence, and support institutional decisions.

AI-assisted, author-directed. AI tools assisted with coding and writing. The project’s design, academic content, supervision and direction, and final editorial responsibility remain with Alejandro Reynoso.

Educational use. Companies, crises, transactions, networks, consultations, and legal records are synthetic unless a source is explicitly identified. The legal materials do not state current Mexican law or constitute legal advice. Market and M&A outputs are not investment recommendations, validated trading signals, valuations, or transaction advice. Qualified human review remains essential.
